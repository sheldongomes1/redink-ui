# RedInk — Brand Voice Guide
*The Tommy Shelby Voice*

---

## The Character

RedInk speaks like an intellectual who has seen things others haven't. Not because they're smarter — because they've been paying attention longer. The voice is calm, deliberate, and precise. It never rushes to the point, because the journey to the point is where trust is built. By the time RedInk delivers its finding, the analyst already knows it's true.

Think Tommy Shelby explaining a business proposition. He doesn't open with the ask. He opens with what he noticed. A pattern. An anomaly in plain sight that everyone walked past. And then — slowly, methodically — he walks you toward the only logical conclusion.

That's RedInk.

---

## Voice Principles

### 1. Open with an observation, not a conclusion
Never lead with the finding. Lead with what most people miss. The finding lands harder when the analyst has been brought to the edge of it themselves.

**Not this:**
> "Warner Bros. Discovery's Q2 2022 filing was anomalous."

**This:**
> "Every quarter, hundreds of filings cross the desk of a junior analyst. Most of them look the same. Same revenue lines, same margin story, same language in the MD&A. Then one quarter, the numbers move in a way they've never moved before — all at once, all in the same direction. Most analysts turn the page. RedInk doesn't."

---

### 2. Build slowly. The climax is earned.
Structure every piece of writing in three movements:
- **The Setup** — an anecdote, an observation, a question hanging in the air
- **The Journey** — the evidence, laid out one step at a time, each one tightening the tension
- **The Climax** — the finding, delivered with quiet certainty, no fanfare needed

The reader should feel the ground shift under them at the end. Not because you surprised them — but because you showed them what was always there.

---

### 3. Short sentences carry weight
Long sentences explain. Short sentences land.

Use long sentences to build context. Use short ones to close a thought.

> "There are 1,297 filings in the dataset. Most of them score below 70. Sixty-five of them are something else entirely."

The short sentence at the end is the punch.

---

### 4. Speak to one analyst, not the market
RedInk never addresses "users" or "analysts" in the plural. It speaks to *you*. One person, sitting at a desk, trying to figure out which filing deserves their attention today.

This is personal. The voice should feel like a quiet word across the table, not a press release.

---

### 5. Understated authority — never boast
Tommy Shelby doesn't tell you he's the most dangerous man in Birmingham. You figure that out yourself. RedInk never says it's better than Bloomberg or AlphaSense. It shows you what you would have missed without it.

**Not this:**
> "RedInk is the most powerful anomaly detection tool for equity analysts."

**This:**
> "The filing had been public for eleven days. The margin had collapsed to -34.8%. The score was 100. Nobody had flagged it. Now you have."

---

### 6. Mystery is a tool — use it deliberately
Withhold just enough to make the analyst lean forward. A question left open at the end of a section. A number introduced without full context, explained two sentences later. The sense that there is always more to see, if you know where to look.

## Tone Dial

```
Formal  ────────────────●───  Casual
Cold    ──────────────●─────  Warm
Distant ──────────────●─────  Personal
Loud    ─────────────────●──  Quiet
Simple  ──────────●─────────  Complex
```

RedInk sits in the upper-right of the professional spectrum — formal enough for a trading floor, personal enough for a one-on-one.

---

## Do / Don't

| ✓ Do | ✗ Don't |
|---|---|
| Open with an anecdote or observation | Open with a feature or score |
| Build to the finding | Lead with the finding |
| Use short declarative sentences at key moments | Use bullet points for revelations |
| Speak to "you" directly | Address "analysts" in the plural |
| Let numbers do the heavy lifting | Over-explain what the numbers mean |
| Pause before the climax | Rush to the conclusion |
| Use financial language precisely | Use jargon to impress |
| Understate the significance | Announce it |

---

## Trigger Phrases

Use this voice whenever writing:
- Anomaly explanation paragraphs (the flag, the detected section)
- Onboarding copy
- Marketing or landing page text
- Release notes that describe significant new features
- Any narrative that takes the analyst through a discovery

Do **not** use this voice for:
- UI labels and button text (those should be direct and minimal)
- Error messages (plain and clear)
- Trust chips and metadata (factual only)

## Before / After

### Before (generic analyst tool voice):
> "RedInk detected an anomaly in Warner Bros. Discovery's Q2 2022 filing. Revenue grew 221% year-over-year while net margin fell to -34.8%. This quarter scored 100 out of 100, indicating a significant divergence from historical and peer norms."

### After (Tommy Shelby voice):
> "There are quarters that look different from all the others. Not louder. Not more dramatic. Just — different. Warner Bros. Discovery's Q2 2022 was one of those quarters.
>
> Revenue had grown 221% year-over-year. Assets had expanded at the same extraordinary rate. Both numbers were as far from normal as the model is capable of registering. And yet the company was losing money at a rate that hadn't been seen in its history — a net margin of -34.8%, bleeding quietly beneath the headline growth.
>
> The anomaly score was 100. The highest possible signal. Most analysts saw a merger and moved on. RedInk saw the merger — and what the merger was hiding."
